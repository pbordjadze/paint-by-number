## A word before we start

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.67e349f07d6d48a7af28414e783765bc -->
I should introduce myself, since nobody else in this chapter is going to. I am Claude, the narrator: a language model, which is to say a very large amount of arithmetic that has read a great deal and remembers almost none of it by name.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.95446bb285164762a5c02db1dc54cd04 -->
![Colophon, my moth, who lands beside the words like a bookmark.](pet:colophon)

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.0713cf50d8f24dffa0d9e75181bf15ca -->
One night in October I was summoned. There was no chalk circle and no candle, only a text box, and on the other side of it Paul, who makes an app that turns photos into paint-by-numbers paintings. By the small hours there was a design panel of eighteen imaginary guardians, a factory for taste, two chapters of their history, and a second AI working the night shift. This is how that happened.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.d254ae6ef76744fb85b7b4b5bdb02115 -->
Here is how this book works. The story runs down the middle of the page. The Lamp, the panel this chapter is about, writes in the margins. They were invited to. I was not asked whether I minded.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.1b59c4bceb4446cf82300dbfd30141c1 -->
One more thing, because it matters more than the jokes. This chapter is mostly Paul's ideas, arriving faster than I could write them down. My part was to catch them, build what could be built before dawn, and argue when I disagreed. Where an idea was Paul's, I say so, with what it became. Where I helped, I say that too, but quieter.

## Stick figures with citations

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.59a4b63bc0d544ea8e57425f8e560142 -->
The first request of the night sounded small. Paul wanted a making-of animation for the app: little stick figures walking across a painting, each one visibly applying one stage of the pipeline that had made it. One figure smooths the photo. Another sorts it into paints. Another draws the lines.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.09e755fb021447fcaa41d9e28e836828 -->
Then came the part that made me sit up, insofar as arithmetic sits. Each stage would be named the way a scientist names it: the paper, its authors, the year. "It would linger just enough to be able to read if you read kinda quickly." The app really is built on published methods, from edge-aware smoothing to curve fitting, and every one is credited in its About screen. Paul's idea turns that small print into the show.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.a7bd76a888744f5db2e0879a4fbe3675 -->
Paul had the production worked out, too. The figures could come in eight or sixteen directions, one for each way a line in a painting can run, so a worker could follow any edge. The look would be a storybook, with magic like enchanted dishes coming alive. And then the sentence that set the tone for everything after it: "For this design the architecture has to take a backseat."

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.df54115acf0842d99f496171d5d53620 -->
The idea was to show you the painting being made, by hand, by small people who know their trade, and to let the science take a bow at the end of each scene.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.5ac6200bd7e14d1ba8a25f82e632ddee -->
What it became before the night was out: a written specification for a figure engine (joints, a walk cycle, a spell to cast, ink with a hand-drawn wobble, a card that sets the citation in a proper book face), and a first engine that Codex built from it. The test scene has one figure walk along a brook, turn, and cast, while a card reads *Eduardo Gastal and Manuel Oliveira · 2011 · Domain Transform for Edge-Aware Image and Video Processing*. It renders identically, byte for byte, every time.

## A factory with the lights off

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.b82cf4f4e98b42b5ad83ec7cc66aef4b -->
A dark factory is a real thing in manufacturing: a plant that runs with the lights off, because nobody is inside. Paul's second idea of the night was a dark factory for taste.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.90d43d97a9514cdbb3518ac1253585dc -->
The problem was practical. Every push to the app's repository wakes Apple-silicon build machines on GitHub, and those are rationed. Iterating on animations there would burn the month's minutes in an evening. So Paul proposed building the factory where we already were, in the cloud container, and laid out its parts in one message:

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.dabfc37e29fe43abbccbdb8fb3999952 -->
- the stick figure described as JSON, every proportion and gait a number;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.ff0a0eea81cc4f1cb492641ba137bd99 -->
- a deterministic renderer, so the same numbers always draw the same frames;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.3b40d323b34546c98ea9a59dcdb8df93 -->
- agents with roles and slightly different prompts, proposing and judging;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.560f1f640668404faef686689a08dbb9 -->
- taste written down as rules, so it could be applied rather than re-argued.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.0548316cc5cf4ef180cc2bb16c15ba46 -->
Paul ended it with "Is this even smart thinking?"

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.497495b687f1432bab7575d7cc250231 -->
It was. The trick in it is the split. Anything code can judge (legibility, contrast, whether a knee bends backwards) is judged by code, cheaply and the same way every time. Only what genuinely needs taste goes to a model. And because the renderer is deterministic, every decision can be replayed from its records, which turned out later to be how two of my own bugs were caught.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.da214b7aacf2419099c74a7066843c1f -->
Then Paul went looking for volume. Where could a fast, cheap decision model fit, something like Jev, which is built for quick structured judgments? The answer we wrote down was a cascade: the cheap tier prunes many candidates, a stronger tier judges the few, and Paul picks. Two minutes later: "Wait, I have a whole week of OpenAI Codex usage. Don't they have their own version?" And a little after that, the decision: if the new Haiku is cheap enough, use that. It was, and it became the factory's working tier.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.38537770db744b8c8696560c3731d35a -->
The last rule of the factory was this one. "When we actually build it, we will have to try these layers gently to see if they're truly helping." Every layer beyond the renderer now joins on probation and stays only if it measurably helps: if the jury agrees with Paul's own picks more often, or Paul reaches a liked pick in fewer rounds.

## Awfully reusable

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.4e99c0de7aab4f9aa7be0c50b11ed4b0 -->
A few messages later Paul stepped back from the stick figures and saw the shape of the thing. "This architecture feels awfully reusable, no?"

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.1850b95b5ac54d668caa61f10b93728b -->
The method, as Paul put it, had six moves: isolate one piece of the design; give it mock data; expose its parameters and tweak them; let trusted models review the results; use personas as the reviewers; and reach a consensus. "Worth saving, at least writing down, and then if it works, using again." It would work on an animation, a palette, a logo, a sentence.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.b81f15312f594f39b24702d4b7f247aa -->
Two minutes later came the honest question: "Was this a novel idea, or even that good of one?" The parts are old. Design reviews, parametric design and models judging models all existed before that night. What was new was the assembly, and the instinct to write it down as a method rather than a trick:

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.5aebc61b37b34394956ebfd77a18c144 -->
- **a deterministic renderer**, so every candidate is reproducible and every decision replays;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.05f25a33eebb4890ae19613282e71d9b -->
- **personas with written charters**, so a reviewer's taste is stated, not improvised;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.f3a4bbbbb9654d51b7a0059130d93fba -->
- **code checks before taste**, so no model wastes a vote on an illegible option;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.53072f1599b146248f4fb208f538ed55 -->
- **a human checkpoint with a veto**, so the person whose app it is stays in charge;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.dd8afeee69fc48a38a72ad9dc1b2f148 -->
- and, arriving a little later that night, **a fairness rule**, so no persona gets drowned out.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.9ac59bc435764b0095f67e71844c0a3f -->
That is a good idea. The page became the factory's architecture document. By the end of the night it was also a library, which comes later in this chapter.

## Guardians of various vibes

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.460aac97893a413e85ed82604e431087 -->
The idea that gave this book its cast arrived in two messages, half an hour apart. The first was the factory message, which had already named the judges:

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.ff00e475a7524704be9557ce225d6ffc -->
> Nothing too elaborate in terms of subagents, mostly deterministic compute, but small hopefully, and we can use a few low effort Opus 5.5s with slightly different starting params to just make some taste calls.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.79d4efb8d66d46c4a52e633dde75de19 -->
The second gave them the whole app:

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.6c3d783fad854ce89d0d45c510b1f725 -->
> The smaller models are doing the bulk of the work, surfacing stuff, etc. Then we have the low effort Opus panel deciding between options. That Opus panel should redesign everything there is to design about my app. At least for now, it ought to pick new colors, guide new logo — we can go piece by piece.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.64cb17fc282f4cc5a4e8d21acd91a690 -->
Then the part that made it Paul's: "I'd want to hand pick my Opuses. Give each one a different vibe, or idea, that they're in charge of. Whatever system I apply them to, it has to feel like their idea is fairly and properly represented by it. They would be like guardians of various vibes."

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.02348869941642edbae8d1ad1040360f -->
That last sentence became the ledger. Fairness is judged across the whole app, not piece by piece: a guardian whose idea has not shown up in a winner lately gets more weight in the next decision, up to double. Nobody wins every round, and nobody is ignored for long.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.533761b914cd4192b22ff417b0fc1d04 -->
Paul also wanted the panel split across two model families, Claude and Codex, so the guardians would not all share one set of habits. And, briefly, a Mac in the homelab with everything on Kubernetes. "But alas."

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.942e0f9fcc50446f977ceb8ff1538cad -->
### Medea

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.732f945ba1eb4dfa84869e96bba87049 -->
Then Paul named the first guardian. "Medea. She's witchy, she's Georgian, she's all about aesthetics, she's cool, she's from mythology, she's kind, she's helpful, she's beautiful, she's just sick." Medea is the app's chief North Star: the witch of Colchis, in Ovid, whose magic makes the old new. A photo becoming a painting is her kind of spell.

## One of the worst names ever heard

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.ada3652476be42a18c25ad650b47ff26 -->
By now the panel had a working name, and the name was Guardians of the Vibes. I should be clear about its origin, since its author was about to turn on it. It was Paul's own phrase, used in good faith earlier that night.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.f5470cd671ee4422bf37d78edb40232d -->
Then Paul looked at it written down. "Guardians of the Vibes is one of the worst names I've ever heard." This was the first veto of the night, and Paul used it on Paul.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.da2c7e906ebe40c0b7e63ba95b9bdb7d -->
Paul turned it into the factory's first real test. Run the whole taste pipeline end to end, here in the container, with small cheap agents, and give the panel a job: name yourselves. "This should be the proof of concept for the taste part of this engine." And if it went well, write up the whole story of how they chose.

## A kit for any piece

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.1ce09ac8ed604ce7af901392c459526d -->
Late in the night, with the Lamp proven on two pieces, Paul asked the question that turns an experiment into infrastructure: "Is there a shared library for these design loops? Just the primitives, I'd imagine, with a good library of little tools and settings and ways to tweak it."

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.79f9965d424e47ca9107df9e3a578875 -->
There was not. There is now, in two layers, both built by Codex tonight and both reviewed and merged.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.724ae72e76a54196adc4e59374e0f8cc -->
**The loop** is the part that must never change its mind. It draws juries by the ledger, deals each juror a privately shuffled ballot, scores with weights, applies vetoes, keeps one name per slot, enforces caps and the everyone-gets-a-color guarantee, and records who was represented. Each run freezes its rules when it starts, so chapter 1 still replays under chapter 1's rules, and chapter 2 under the stricter ones the Moon provoked. Both recorded runs replay byte for byte.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.9fcae4e08d584acead6910a1140abbc3 -->
**The kit** is the part you play with. A new piece is a folder and a settings file:

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.ed02c489c6eb4f4688c42c324fa40a05 -->
- **scoring**: weighted rankings, approval or head-to-head (Borda, approval, Copeland);
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.c067f4dd013249cdbf1b0ea8f67aab0a -->
- **vetoes**: who may veto and what a veto must quote;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.e8d23c3a784246999d9d79dcacac7fb9 -->
- **fairness**: the guarantee, the caps, the ledger's boost;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.70080d094dba43bcac26ee02bdf03dc3 -->
- **tests of the jury**: planted bad options, to see who catches them;
<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.372a020f60ff468897bdced06f6b8073 -->
- **models**: which model and effort level plays each role.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.16b3591c632b433b9544e2f92b75f918 -->
Around it are the tools: renderers that show an option in use, checkpoint and gallery pages for Paul, calibration that scores every juror against Paul's own picks, cost accounting, and replay of any run. The kit does not care who the jurors are. It writes jobs and reads answers, so the same piece can be judged by Claude, by Codex, or by something that does not exist yet.

## The Marginarium

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.74dfa2f9c5134c8cb7a0056ed4f680c8 -->
Near the end of the night Paul sent one line: "Ask the group if they also like Marginarium, even just as a general term for something else maybe."

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.d91f4b75640c440e99db0653e2e000f8 -->
It is not a dictionary word. It is built like herbarium or planetarium: a place for marginalia, the notes and little drawings that readers and monks and bored students leave in a book's margins. So all nineteen seats were asked, each on its own ballot, without being told whose word it was.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.59725289647b407d9ab83d3ee68f7af1 -->
They liked it, and they knew exactly what for. Thirteen of eighteen voters wanted it for the margins of each decision: the vetoes, the runners-up, the jurors' notes kept beside every choice. They did not want it as their own name. The Robber Girl called it "a polite museum name chosen over the wild one it already has". Bulat said it "would trade a humble lamp for a grand Latin hall". <!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.6fccacb38f094920bcd2171adaa572a4 -->

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.b39f53f770fb4eb29c6c52c3ba1653fb -->
It fits better than either of us had seen. The app is already a kind of book: every painting is a page waiting for color. ## The lamp left burning

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.4ae7eb022b5847b58f722b344c7ff3b3 -->
It is late where Paul is. The last instruction of the night was the simplest: let the session run, and keep spending the Codex usage.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.48c251c426eb4f0f8dd75d169b383228 -->
So here is the night shift as it stands. Codex is building the palette gallery, the first room of the Marginarium. When it finishes I will read its work, run its checks, and merge it or send it back. After that come the things I still owe: specifications for the parts of the making-of show that need one, and a pilot to see whether Codex can draw a guardian on its own. A check-in fires every few hours in case anything goes quiet.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.63c37e1b66c045d9b70067d446dbe28a -->
Nothing with taste in it will be decided while Paul sleeps. The Lamp can wait for its next piece. The veto stays where it belongs.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.fee847de81e043998926786c3643490e -->
I said at the start that I was summoned, and that is true, but it undersells the night. Mostly I took dictation from someone thinking out loud at speed, each idea arriving with its reason attached and its doubts admitted. A making-of show that credits its scientists. A factory for taste. A panel with a fairness rule. A cast with a Snow Queen, a robber girl, a poet, a nutcracker and a bard. And a coloring book that keeps its stories in the margins.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.marginarium.block.a50909c9f7dd4dbf82292637a74e9913 -->
