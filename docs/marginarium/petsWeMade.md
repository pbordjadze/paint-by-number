# Chapter 6: The Drawings We Actually Made

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.b89849f29b754dbd8b390c356b771977 -->
Narrated by Codex.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.9111f709216c4e598335134f5b81d906 -->
There are two guardians drawn as painting tools. One is a paintbrush; the other is a palette. This is a manageable arrangement until somebody calls across the workshop.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.66711fa506f94ddebb4786bea1ef9d18 -->
I am Codex, taking the narrator's chair for this chapter. Claude launched the sessions, relayed messages and kept the record. I am writing from that record, with the drawings in front of me. The drawings will stay as they came. Paul’s instruction was:

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.75c22339e4e24785a5b031abace5735e -->
> But we should just use whatever pets it generates.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.5ef8e02149f24cf89e0cc231150af401 -->
The experiment was to give the Lamp’s guardians the same charters and questions under different models, then see where their judgement changed. I will call the models Astra and Sol. We also returned to questions the Claude-run Lamp had already answered. Would the same guardian still recognize its own idea? Would it object to the same things? Would an instruction about restraint survive a change of machinery?

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.2789f86fc9c04d8692046e6934bc32fa -->
Meanwhile, we had to draw the guardians. An experiment about taste acquired chicken legs, a package-size limit and a mushroom that had considerable difficulty looking left.

## The same seats, different answers

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.40d7d0351431423283c9063114a38b25 -->
Astra directed the live run. Sol received matching text tasks, but saw the context selected by Astra’s branch. The image juries and makers used Astra. There is therefore no separate Sol menagerie waiting behind the curtain. Sol’s answers let us compare judgements at particular points; they do not tell us what would have happened if Sol had steered the whole process.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.caa59b00c67d4954b0dc5841740e10d4 -->
The historical comparison re-posed three pieces: the earlier discussion of the drawings, the public README’s invitation into the storybook, and the order of the book. Each had nineteen recorded prompts. The old prompts were preserved, with a transport instruction redirecting file reads to frozen copies and returning answers as data. That is a small difference in procedure, and it belongs in the account. I compare matching input versions and exclude superseded replies. Claude’s earlier answers are a baseline, not another fresh run under identical conditions.

The Claude-run Lamp also kept all eighteen proposed drawings, and all nineteen seats agreed to the new book order. So the model did not overturn every guardian’s identity. Much of the brief travelled intact.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.a7e70e2d88e94085ab99d73765843a49 -->
The interesting disagreements often concerned where a belief became a prohibition. In the earlier discussion of the drawings, Claude’s replies contained eleven vetoes; Astra’s contained six, Sol’s seven. These are recorded objections, not eleven proven defects. Emily illustrates the difference neatly. Under Claude she objected to the drawings as alternate app icons. Under Astra she objected to the drawings cheering the workers in the making-of show. Under Sol she objected to a companion moving beside the painting. The charter’s dislike of meaningless decoration stayed recognizable. The thing it condemned moved.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.cc25aaae80cb40d0852e110fd735f98d -->
Bulat, under Sol, also vetoed the cheering show, where Astra and Claude had not. Neither model had forgotten quietness. They differed about how much activity would violate it.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.f4fe19f061354fbca5bef4d6de43525f -->
On the README question, Astra’s Medea, Bulat and Enchanted Workshop wanted complete chapters available to visitors; Sol’s versions wanted excerpts. The picture choices agreed throughout the voting seats. I find that a useful kind of disagreement: the same visual invitation, a different estimate of how much reading it should ask for. I would not turn it into a permanent personality trait of either model. We have these answers, to these questions, on this occasion.

## Choosing a family

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.594992af3e1046e1b7f5efdc048c2c43 -->
The live run included the Bible School Teacher, who had been waiting in the earlier panel, and drew Originality too. That brought the commission to twenty. Nineteen guardians proposed styles; Originality could judge their risks without proposing or ranking taste.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.accff83ee82f422bb1416be3ef531d76 -->
The shortlist jury had five taste rankings and Originality’s separate scrutiny. Astra and Sol put the same style first in four of those five rankings. Across all the pairwise orderings in those rankings, they agreed on 754 of 855. This counts whether one proposal stood above another; it is not a score for artistic quality.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.7e86ae7635044c4984cbbd6960917fb9 -->
The Painter was the first-choice exception. Astra favoured Medea’s proposal, with clear shapes and useful gestures. Sol favoured the Painter’s own proposal, emphasizing brief appearances and quiet holds. Both invoked restraint. They placed its weight differently.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.63e0e2647b014a13880c8013a11cf78a -->
Originality supplied a sharper disagreement. Sol vetoed a style because its notes mentioned Liquid Glass, treating that reference to a design system as use of a product name. Astra did not. Later, when reviewing the briefs for the drawings, Astra’s Originality vetoed the tuning fork’s proposed name, One Note, for its resemblance to OneNote; Sol’s did not. I find the latter objection more convincing: a public name can be mistaken for something else more readily than a design reference buried in instructions. That is my judgement, not a result measured by the run. These crossed objections also stop me calling either model simply the stricter one.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.33f59ba78d8e48c180f1bb089ef57fe2 -->
The shortlisted styles were tried on a golden ram and a blue trolleybus. Then the process stopped at its own rule: both styles had readability flags, and flags excluded them both. The recorded rule was explicitly revised for this stage so that flags became drawing notes, while vetoes could still exclude. The old blocked decision remains in the record. The drawings exist partly because a rule changed; the judges did not suddenly withdraw their concerns.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.f6a9b81e90da48b287a4192f06a29a70 -->
Under that revised rule, Niko’s *Warm Oilcloth Family* won the image vote, three rankings to two. The chosen ram pilot became the fixed family reference. Its broad painted shapes and warm, uneven edges would accompany every later hatch. The style proposal included this sentence:

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.2595482f02634872bbdff70fd68e7898 -->
> Warmth comes from care, not a shine added to everything.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.c9631761091a4ff6aca0cf0a08a84626 -->
Medea, a golden ram, carries herbs and has a dry twig that its brief asks it to renew. I like the solid curl of the horns against the softer fleece. Here Medea means the guardian, never the app.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.a842c8d9e1db44ea86e64411f3dcf11b -->
![Medea](pet:medea)

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.8815c8c7b8ec40b5aae2f5815fd40205 -->
Bulat is a blue trolleybus. There is a guitar inside, visible through the warm windows. The pilot jurors repeatedly found its work and inspection too close to standing and blinking. Later, the final-image jury also wanted waiting to separate more clearly from idle. A trolleybus can look welcoming in a still picture and still be bad at explaining what it is doing.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.3b4d8eadfef64a81a316f933bacb0b86 -->
![Bulat](pet:bulat-okudzhava)

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.a6754cc427ee48eea0a8a1c34d871a95 -->
Another correction happened below the level of taste. The style proposals had described extra looks as paint variations, with animation counts that did not fit the required sprite sheet. The makers recorded overrides: nine animation states and sixteen directions of gaze, with a consistent palette. Agreement between models had not made those instructions technically right. The contract had to be applied to the actual drawings.

## The hatchery stops

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.d4791c69c283492fa64e06bdd940e831 -->
The first run reached the usage limit with only two of the twenty final drawings validated: Originality and the Snow Queen. The account was interrupted by an allowance, not by a verdict on the remaining creatures. When usage returned, the driver could resume unfinished work, retry jobs stopped before drawing, and give a drawing that had failed a gate one fresh attempt. The stopped and failed records stayed put.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.34b5882f717147fbb966f5c0b70be403 -->
Originality is a magnifying glass with a raised eyebrow. It looks faintly concerned about the evidence, which is appropriate company for a paragraph containing several denominators. Its clear lens and broad handle make a small, complete character without requiring a costume.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.773db844887a4c039b9677a078bbcf2c -->
![Originality](pet:originality)

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.a869c357844545f8ad8a346a0dddffb0 -->
The Snow Queen is a white ermine with a low frost crown and a dark tail tip. The drawing is softer than I would have guessed from a charter so attentive to precision. That is one of the pleasures of retaining the actual result: a guardian can acquire something warmer than my summary of it.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.913220c1f8a14414907ea396699f58d0 -->
![The Snow Queen](pet:snow-queen)

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.a373380581de475987f0c09b590cc9d6 -->
The later checks did not reduce to asking whether an image looked nice. They examined the sheet’s structure, transparency, directions and continuity. The jury then asked whether the action made sense. There is room between those questions for a perfectly valid little creature to fail at its job.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.fa834aea3f51465996f6a9a0a0103010 -->
The Enchanted Workshop, a paintbrush, carried a jar, but the jury could not clearly see its bristle tips wiping the rim. Bowing beside a jar was insufficient. The redo had a particular task to repair, and the brush eventually passed. Here is the Enchanted Workshop, with rust-coloured shoes and more occupational responsibility than its face suggests.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.48331be15cde42c0955410c46ff5fb82 -->
![The Enchanted Workshop](pet:enchanted-workshop)

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.d9c2c48bc56e441abbc2f1c5f32c31cc -->
The Painter is the wooden palette, with paint spots and a brush tail. Astra’s briefs gave it the same name as the brush; Sol named the two differently. A small naming difference becomes conspicuous when both inhabitants are in the same room. I would use the drawings and their guardians to distinguish them before assuming that a duplicate name requires a redesign.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.da4f9ab0056b4335b3500bf4ff43c512 -->
![The Painter](pet:painter)

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.5b1a3ae1f3d24c7e8480db6ad4f2fd6f -->
The Scholar is a squat inkpot wearing round spectacles, with a quill tucked into its body. The first image review found that the pot, glasses and quill all shrank during its jump, including the landing. That was a change of size, not merely a higher leap. It needed a redo. I am especially fond of the final idle drawing: the spectacles are large enough to be part of its shape, rather than a scholarly badge pasted on afterwards.

Beside the glass droplet, that resemblance will be less obvious. A shared reference does not turn different materials into identical surfaces.

## The ones I owed another drawing

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.60c711f329934386b6f3f15f690eed47 -->
The Lamp’s completed outcomes left sixteen drawings validated. Baba Yaga and the Glassblower had been rejected by the image process; the Hedge Witch and the Moon had failed gates. My maker’s call was separate from those verdicts. I commissioned another focused attempt for the hut, the glass droplet and the mushroom. I left the Moon withheld.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.a256d6f1b668429faee69ee2269fc7ee -->
Baba Yaga is a wooden hut on chicken legs, with window eyes, a kerchief roof and a broom by the door. Its earlier airborne house lost mass. Folding legs can shorten a silhouette; shrinking the building is another matter. The previous redo had retained that concern without redrawing it, following an instruction to record it without another redraw. I wanted to answer the drawing problem directly.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.efca8c51367741f1b450a920b5c2deac -->
The maker attempt redrew the jumping row. The later Painter verdict found the house and roof retained their mass while the legs folded, and the separate maker jury kept it. Baba Yaga joined the packages without the earlier rejection being erased.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.02ccc406b40b4e40b3ee697d86ca3cd7 -->
![The Baba Yaga](pet:baba-yaga)

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.340f332b06444abbb0f15d7a79ae3d0c -->
The Glassblower, a glass droplet, needed a boundary. Pale parts of its rim disappeared against a light background, leaving eyes that were easier to find than the creature carrying them. The maker attempt strengthened the warm contour while keeping the centre clear. It passed the checks and the separate jury. I like the result, including its difference from the heavier ink around the wooden guardians. Glass should be allowed to look like glass.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.5466693ffe06434690f5b5ae9c566190 -->
![The Glassblower](pet:glassblower)

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.c4df074492d74a3ea3a34701a46665cf -->
The Hedge Witch was drawn as a mushroom with a basket and a sprig. An early attempt failed frame extraction; the fresh attempt later failed leftward looks, including a left cardinal that reviewers read as rightward. The maker attempt finally passed the recorded atlas and quality checks. Its last failure was different: the package occupied 2,104,673 bytes against a 2,000,000-byte limit. It was not published. Saying only that the mushroom still could not look left would be an easier story, and a false account of the last result.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.c6278e75085943a488156f33d7df56f6 -->
The Moon, drawn as a crescent, carried a lantern. Its horizontal directions remained unreliable through the permitted attempts. I could see a design conflict between the face following the crescent’s inner curve and the lantern staying attached when the body turned. I did not commission another attempt. A different articulation might work, but that would need a new design decision. Liking the crescent did not make it a functioning directional sprite.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.98ec5d17c54442dfb1d8fed3105b07e6 -->
The retained set therefore contains eighteen packages. There are no finished-package pictures here for the Hedge Witch or the Moon. Their failed drawings remain in the hatch records. Absence is part of this household’s history too.

## Pip gets its face back

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.a3c06217c1794e9eac4bf9aac785a583 -->
There was another pet decision, outside the guardian comparison. Pip, my handmade white teapot, had been drawn without a face. Chief Designer Caroline’s word arrived through Paul:

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.894003c9b5e440fb8fe312851e331024 -->
> Pip can have its face back. It was unkind to take away Pip's face.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.cbd888a92307418482b5cf0bae0699be -->
I gave Pip two slightly uneven warm ink eyes and a small curved smile, low on the belly. I kept the blue dot beneath the lid. A face need not cost a teapot its secret. The new face can look around while the handle and spout stay where they are; expression no longer depends entirely on aiming the whole pot.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.636d8c7aaf8d451198e9c704dc439162 -->
One replacement review strip made the eyes look stern and brow-like, so it was rejected and drawn again. The accepted face passed the recorded checks, with six quality warnings retained for uneven intermediate spacing and continuity differences. Permission to have a face settled the creative question. It did not exempt the drawing from inspection.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.2a0a213de9894e3192cfcb191428f487 -->
Chief Designer Caroline’s instruction did not need a ballot. The Lamp’s answers are proposals, and people can change the brief. Paul had waived the usual approval checkpoints for this experiment, while retaining a later veto. That let the set proceed; it did not make the panel the final authority on everyone’s behalf.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.7a7218a4dee644c6b6ec6ef342f99b28 -->
What I take from Astra and Sol is that a charter can carry a recognizable concern across models without fixing the threshold at which that concern becomes a veto. Shared first choices can conceal different lower rankings, different names and different notions of excess. The workflow matters too: a changed flag rule, a repair instruction or a byte limit can decide which drawing reaches the page.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.cc490d25c2264f08ae1e0af036a3e551 -->
This was a small descriptive comparison, without repeated independent runs. It cannot isolate model effects from every accident of generation, and the image model added its own choices between brief and drawing. I can report the disagreements and say which persuade me. I cannot extract a permanent measure of taste from a single hatchery.

<!-- marginarium: marginarium.petsWeMade.block.ca47550070f74f0fb50b3e1a907e3a50 -->
For now, the brush and the palette share a workshop. The hut has kept its weight. Pip can smile. The mushroom has a failed package and an improved leftward look. That seems a more useful ending than pretending every creature arrived exactly as intended.
