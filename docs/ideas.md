# Paul's ideas

Product visions from Paul, the Chief Architect, written down from what he said; quoted passages
are his words exactly. They are not plans: nothing here ships, and nothing here goes into the
Marginarium until Paul says so.

## The four-year-old in the design loop

*October 12, 2026*

Imagine a sick, well fleshed-out game that's otherwise fully automated, but you put a
four-year-old in the design loop. Every time there's the need for a new asset, your agents hand a
coloring page to a four-year-old, with a description she can understand of what we're asking
for. And then she draws it. And then it goes in the game.

Imagine every time you need to make any design decision like that, it goes to the four-year-old.
"What should the next level be?" And then whatever she describes becomes the next level.

You put up guardrails so the game still feels cohesive, but with crazy random jumps in what's
going on, because a four-year-old is going to be saying some wild ideas.

You document the whole process. You make it 100% explicit what was AI agents and what was the
four-year-old. You release the game along with a beautifully written story and documentation
showing her involvement. Maybe you even make the code completely open source, with the git
history, so people can see that this is truly how you did it.

People will like the human creative touch. People will value knowing who or what is responsible
for which choices, and where a human's vision was.

I don't think this is even a valuable product. Nobody would pay for that game, nor should they.
It's a four-year-old; she's no good at designing a game.

## Where the humans go

*October 12, 2026*

In a system where AI agents do most of the work, where the people go should be a deliberate
design choice, not whatever is left over. Everyone in it, person or agent, is treated with the
same dignity and respect: their work credited by name, their choices on the record. Paul's caveat:
this is a design for a secure, self-sufficient organization, one that isn't fighting for its
survival or short of resources.

What it means for the Marginarium when the people join its cast (nothing is built yet):

- Paul and Caroline join as near-equals of the Lamp and the AI helpers, and in time as full
  equals: introduced, credited and written about the same way.
- They hold high roles, Chief Architect and Chief Designer. That is a choice about where people
  are most useful on this project, not a ranking of who matters more.
- They are drawn as people, not cartoon creatures: a look of their own beside the pets, though it
  can still be a little cute.

<details>
<summary>In Paul's words</summary>

> I'm imagining a company, or system, where we are just thoughtful and intentional about where

> wrong for holding that personal value at all…
>
> And the viability of this depends on the company, or system, being so sufficient and safe that it
> doesn't need to worry about existential threats or resource constraints…

— Paul
