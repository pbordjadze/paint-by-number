# Paul's directive: the app's leverage (October 17, 2026)

His words: "when they do get to the app screens — THEY SHOULD FOCUS ON THE “PAINTING YOUR OWN
BEAUTIFUL FAMILY PICTURES” or “PAINT AND SEND YOUR PETS” — that is our whole leverage. So you will
need to find pictures of families and pets that fit our brief—beautiful intimate pictures that might
remind you of family and friends. Please use stock photography, but nice and cute — obviously, a
taste call, so the studio still runs, just make sure they’re aware of this directive. If they
absolutely hate it we don’t have to use it. And make sure, if they use it, we keep it diverse, and
showcase different ethnicities, gender identities, body size, etc., in the marketing pictures, even
though it should be ethically sourced stock photography."

For the name and icon: this is the app's positioning, not only a screenshot note. Your own people,
painted by you (your family, your pets), and sent to them. A name and an icon can carry that, or
deliberately leave it to the screens: either is fine; say which you chose and why.

For the screens (a later round): real app screens painting ethically sourced, model-released stock
photos of families and pets, chosen for warmth and intimacy, and diverse across ethnicity, gender
identity, body size, age, disability and family shape, never as tokens. The studio may argue
against it; Paul said "If they absolutely hate it we don’t have to use it."

Paul's option, later the same day: "What if I use pictures and photos of my own family and friends,
that they sign off on?" The screens round considers these first, before stock: real family photos
are the leverage itself. Requirements (Claude's, for Paul): a written release from everyone
identifiable (App Store and marketing use, the photo and its painted versions, worldwide, for as
long as the app is sold; withdrawal means replacement in the next update); a parent or guardian for
children; the photographer's permission when it isn't Paul; location data stripped; no identifiable
bystanders, house numbers or logos; no real names on screen (neutral painting titles); originals
kept out of the public repo. Ethically sourced stock fills gaps only.
