# Correction from Paul: "Fern perfumes" is a company

Paul: "Fern perfumes — there's just a company called fern that have good branding". The brand is
Ffern (double f), a small British perfume house. The fougère reading in your brief was Claude's,
and wrong: drop it as Paul's intent (keep "evoke, don't illustrate" only if your own work earns it).
Take Ffern as the bar for branding, as people who get it.

What can be verified (October 2026):
- Four small-batch natural perfumes a year: "we'll blend you a bottle of perfume for each season,
  delivered at the solstice or equinox" (ffern.co). Named plainly by season and year: "Autumn 26".
- A membership with a waitlist ("Register with our concierge to check availability"), a seasonal
  parcel, and artwork with each season that reviewers call "worth framing".
- Voice: measured and quiet: "Natural, limited-edition, blended in the English countryside."
- Plastic-free paper packaging.
- Its look, from a third-party write-up (not an official Ffern document, so treat as approximate):
  earthy and folkloric, a serif feel, film photography and paper collage, a muted natural palette
  that shifts with the season.

What that adds up to: one idea held everywhere (the sky's calendar: solstice and equinox), the
product named plainly, restraint in the mark, and art doing the emotional work. It is not a style
to copy: no borrowing Ffern's name, marks or look. Originality would veto that.
