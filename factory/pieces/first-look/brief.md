# The Lamplit Table's first look: what hasn't shipped

**Paint by Moonlight** is an iPhone and iPad app that turns a photo into a paint-by-numbers page
you paint by tapping its areas. The Lamplit Table is the Lamp's standing jury for the app's
screens and controls: the Astrologer, Rikyū, the Child at Bedtime, the Hedge Witch, Madeleine and
Originality, who can only veto. Paul, the app's Chief Architect, October 17, 2026: "use this
specific lamp to run and oversee all future UI features, starting with all the ones that haven't
shipped yet."

These are the six. Most were built today by other Claude sessions; the Table looks them over
before they ship. Its answers are proposals: Paul decides, and nothing is changed on another
session's work without him. The pictures are screenshots from the iPad build (13-inch); open each
with the Read tool. Judge only what your charter cares about; "ship" is a fine answer.

## 1. Sun and Moon (your own first piece, as built)

Settings › Sound & Haptics gains Sky: Follow the Sky (the default), Sun, Moon. Follow the Sky is
Sun from 6 a.m. to 7 p.m. by the phone's own clock, Moon the rest. By Sun painting plays the
kalimba a little brighter and lighter, a tone higher; by Moon a celesta-like bell on the same five
notes, lower, as their minor, a little uneven as if made by hand, with a quiet twinkle an octave up
where a phrase rests. Finishing a painting by Moon: a slower run down to a warm low chord, one star
left ringing. By Moon the two gold sparkles that pop when an area fills are eight-point stars.

- Settings (no picture yet: its build is waiting for a test machine): a Sky row whose icon is the
  sky that holds now (a sun, or a crescent with stars), a menu of the three choices, each with its
  symbol, and under it: "Sun and Moon each have their own music, and by Moon the sparkles are
  stars. Follow the Sky turns to Moon at 7 PM and back to Sun at 6 AM, your time."
- `sky-star.jpg`: the day's sparkle (left) and the night's star (right), large and at size, drawn
  from the app's own shapes. The two grounds are only for the picture: the sky changes no paper
  (Settings › Paper does that).

## 2. Gentle tips

The first visit to the gallery asks: **"Small Tips?** Would you like small tips as you go? You can
change this in Settings." with Show Tips and No Thanks of equal weight; closing counts as No Thanks,
and it never asks again. Settings gets a Tips switch. With tips on, at most one tip at a time, three
minutes apart with something done between, three a session, five a day, three on the first
painting, and none while a finger is on the page, a fill lands, or the Making-of, a replay, Zen or
feedback is on. Each tip has Turn Off Tips under its close button and goes for good once its thing
is used. The tips, title then words (no pictures yet):

- A Page Is Waiting: Tap a page in Ready to Paint to begin. Nothing needs setting up first.
- Make Your Own Page: Tap + to turn one of your photos, or a sample, into a page to paint.
- More for Each Painting: Touch and hold a painting to favorite, rename, share, print or start it over.
- Share the Making: Touch and hold a finished painting and choose Share for a time-lapse of it.
- Settings and Tips: The gear holds paper tone, sound and painting length. Tips can be turned off there.
- No Photo at Hand: Samples has paintings and photographs ready to use.
- Compare Photo and Page: Drag the divider to compare the photo and the page. Pinch to look closer.
- Already Chosen for You: These settings were picked for this photo. You can just tap Start Painting.
- You Can Always Go Back: Watch the preview change. Reset to Suggested returns every slider to where it started.
- Fewer or More Areas: Fewer colors and less detail make a calmer page. More makes a longer, finer one.
- Shape the Lines Yourself: Refine, top right, lets you draw or erase lines and fill shapes. It is optional.
- Make a Shape Its Own: Draw a closed shape, then fill it to make it an area with its own number.
- Tap to Paint: Pick a color below, then tap an area marked with its number to fill it.
- Can't Find It?: Tap the selected color again, or the lightbulb, to be shown an area to paint.
- Zoom In on Small Areas: Double-tap the page to zoom in. Pinch to zoom back out.
- Paint Several Areas: Touch and hold, then drag across areas with this number to fill them in one stroke.
- Paint with Apple Pencil: The Pencil paints while fingers move the page. Double-tap the Pencil for the next color.
- Peek at the Photo: Hold Show Photo to see the original. Tap it to keep it showing.
- Watch It Again: Replay Painting plays your page back from the first fill.
- A Calmer Way to Paint: In More, Zen Mode moves the page to the next area after each fill.
- Tell Us What You Think: In More, Give Feedback lets you mark the page and say what is wrong or right.
- Worth a Look Here: Painting Length sets how long new pages take. The Making-of shows a photo becoming its page.

## 3. The painting's back chevron

On the painting screen, the button that returns to the gallery was an X; now it is the system's
back chevron, as the painting sits on the gallery's stack. VoiceOver says Back.

- `paint-x-top.jpg`: the painting screen's top bar today; `paint-chevron-top.jpg`: with the
  chevron. `paint-chevron.jpg`: the whole screen.

## 4. Start Painting as one motion

Today, tapping Start Painting slides the create screen down, the gallery flashes, then the new
painting zooms in from its card: two unrelated motions. Now the painting opens under the create
screen, which dissolves into it while the preview picture glides to where the painting's page
sits, in about half a second with a gentle spring. (Motion only: no pictures.)

## 6. Replay the making-of (not built yet)

The Making-of (Settings › The Making-of, off by default) plays a short show after Start
Painting: the painter's own photo becoming its page, step by step, about 20 seconds; a tap skips
it. It can't be watched again yet. The plan from October was "replay from More", the painting's
menu (which already holds Replay Painting, the time-lapse of the painter's own fills). Design it:
where it lives, and what it is called.
