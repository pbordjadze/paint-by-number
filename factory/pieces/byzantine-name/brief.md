# A name for the Byzantine workshop

## What Paul asked

October 23, 2026, after seeing a prototype that turns any photo into a mosaic of glass tiles laid
in rows that follow the picture's lines:

> "This Mosaic proposal is incredible and I really like it. Can we keep heading down this
> path—but—this is a bold choice here, can you actually make it a separate app on SideStore
> please, for now at least?"

> "I think it shares a lot of the same mechanisms but almost should be like, a crazy hidden
> feature Easter egg deep in the bowels of the app, so I'd want to finish it first and make it
> good before even like, really telling anyone."

> "...somewhere VERY hidden, another 'time change' setting, that takes us ALLLL the way back to
> some era (you said Byzantine?)"

> "Then it would be a whole new app. It would share the general structure but actually
> everything would be different—there would be a whole new design language, the 'making of'
> screen should show the process of building the mosaic with like, techniques named and credited
> to mosaic artists, the animations could be different, it might not even look like paint going
> down, maybe it would look like tiles being placed."

And then: "For working name, can you do like a tasteful lamp session? ... pick the ones who
already are ancient and have them name the new branch, please. But like, don't overdo it."

## What is being named

A working name for the new project: its code repository on GitHub (public, so the name is a
short lowercase slug) and, for now, the separate app Paul installs from SideStore to try it. It
is a working name, not the final one: one day this becomes a secret era hidden inside Paint by
Moonlight, opened by turning a hidden clock back to Byzantium. Nobody else will see the name for
a while.

The project: a photo becomes a mosaic. Its outlines are laid first as single rows of dark tiles;
every other tile follows them in rows (the mosaicist's *andamento*); each tile takes one of a few
glass colors (*smalti*); the painter then sets the tiles one by one. The touchstones are the
sixth-century mosaics of Ravenna and Constantinople: gold grounds, faces in tiny tesserae, eyes
set as single round pieces.

## Candidates

a. **Andamento**: the mosaicist's word for the flow of the rows.
b. **Smalti**: the glass tiles of Byzantine mosaics.
c. **Ravenna**: the city whose sixth-century mosaics are the touchstone.
d. **Gold Ground**: the gold field behind Byzantine figures.
e. **Musivum**: the Latin for wall mosaic (*opus musivum*, work of the Muses).
f. **Mosaic by Moonlight**: the sister app's name, plainly.
g. **Tesserae**: the tiles themselves.
h. **547**: the year San Vitale in Ravenna was consecrated, and the year the hidden clock
   would be set to.

Or a name of your own.
