# Chapter 4: the brief every guardian reads

Paul just bought a used M1 MacBook Air for $40. When it arrives, Claude can run Xcode and the iOS
Simulator on it, which means **the Lamp can finally use the app it has been designing.**

The app (called Medea from now on; Paint by Moonlight today) turns a photo into a paint-by-numbers
painting on the device. You pick a photo or a picture from its library of public-domain paintings;
it makes a coloring-book template, with lines, numbered areas and a palette whose paints have little
nicknames. Then you paint: tap or drag with a finger or a pencil to fill each numbered area with its
paint. There is a Refine step to draw or erase lines before painting, suggested settings for how
long a painting should take, printing to PDF, VoiceOver, dark mode, and Apple's Liquid Glass look.

With the Mac, a guardian will be able to look at a screenshot of the app, choose a move (tap here,
drag there, pick paint 12, open Settings), see the result, and keep going. It can paint a whole
picture, record a video of it, or run the app's demo scenes.

Chapter 4 is about what the Lamp will do with that. Paul has asked for it to be plain and warm: say
what you mean, a light touch is welcome, no puns.
