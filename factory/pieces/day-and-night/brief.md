# Day and Night: the brief every juror reads

**Paint by Moonlight** is an iPhone and iPad app that turns a photo into a paint-by-numbers
painting; you paint it by tapping its areas. This is the first piece for a new standing jury of
the Lamp (A Lamp Left Burning, the app's design panel of guardians, each with a written charter).

## The jury

Paul, the app's Chief Architect, chose its six seats on October 17, 2026. From now on they run
and oversee every new screen and control of the app, starting with the ones that haven't shipped
yet:

- **The Astrologer**, new to the Lamp. She leads this piece.
- **Rikyū**, who guards minimalism.
- **The Child at Bedtime**, who guards wonder and warmth, never fear.
- **The Hedge Witch**, who guards the warmth of things grown by hand.
- **Madeleine**, after the writer Madeleine L'Engle, new to the Lamp, who takes the Bible School
  Teacher's seat on the Hearth: a very gentle guardian of wholesomeness, "but a super chill one".
- **Originality**, who can only veto, and never votes on taste.

The rest of the Lamp keep their turns on everything else (names, colors, drawings, chapters).

## The feature, in Paul's words

> There should be different types of music for dark mode or light mode. Like brighter daytime
> music for light mode, more sparkly and whimsigoth nighttime for night. And—do we have timezone
> localization? Then we can have it switch between them based on time of day of the user. And we
> can call it "Day" or "Night" or like "Moon" or "Sun"—just a very very gentle note of astrology
> there, like a subtle nod, and then in Day mode there should be a more bright, lighter, verrrry
> minimal astrological vibe, and the same thing for night. Think midsummer nights dream—but with
> an edgy astrological vibe.

## What the app does today

- **The music.** Every area painted plays the next note of one gentle tune, whatever its color,
  so any painting order sounds like the same melody. The notes are a plucked, kalimba-like tone
  on a five-note (pentatonic) scale, so overlapping notes never clash. Finishing a color plays a
  little rising three-note arpeggio; finishing the painting plays a run up every note and a soft
  chord. A wrong-color tap is a muted wooden thock.
- Everything is synthesized on the device: no recordings. The sounds mix with the painter's own
  music and respect the silent switch.
- **Settings › Sound & Haptics** has one switch, Sounds (on by default).
- **The look.** The app's identity is already moonlit: its name, native iOS with a darker,
  slightly witchy personality, Nightshade (a deep purple) and antique gold, a gold crescent, two
  gold sparkles when an area fills. Gold is kept for stars, sparkles and the crescent. The
  painting screen keeps its chrome quiet so the painting comes first.
- **The time.** The phone's clock already keeps the painter's own time zone, so the app can know
  whether it is day or night where they are with no location and no permission.

## What Claude proposes, for you to change

- Two sets of sounds: a Day set and a Night set, each with its own tone, tune, finished-color
  flourish and finale. The thock stays the same in both.
- A choice in Settings, under Sounds: automatic (by the painter's local time), always Day, or
  always Night. Automatic is the default.
- A change of sky never cuts a phrase: it waits for the tune's next phrase to begin.
- The tunes are our own; nothing is borrowed from any piece of music.
- No horoscopes, no zodiac signs, nothing about the painter's fate: the astrology is only the
  sun and the moon keeping the hours.

## Your questions

1. **Names.** What do the two choices and the automatic choice say in Settings (each at most
   three words)? Paul offered "Day" and "Night", or "Sun" and "Moon".
2. **The hours.** When does automatic turn from night to day and back? For example 6 a.m. and
   6 p.m. all year, or 7 and 7, or an hour you name.
3. **Day's sound.** Paul asked for "brighter daytime music". Pick one: (a) today's kalimba
   tune, unchanged; (b) today's kalimba, a little brighter and lighter; (c) something new (say
   what, in at most 12 words). One sentence on its tune.
4. **Night's sound.** "More sparkly and whimsigoth." Pick a voice: (a) a celesta-like bell with
   a faint shimmer, (b) a music box, (c) a glass harmonica (glass rims, eerie and soft), (d) other
   (at most 12 words). Pick a scale: (a) the same five notes as the day, centred lower, as its
   minor (the same stars, another sky); (b) a darker scale with half-steps, knowing that
   overlapping notes will rub. Should some notes twinkle: a quiet echo above them, like a star?
5. **The visual nod.** "Verrrry minimal." Pick at most one: (a) nothing beyond Settings, where
   each choice has its symbol (a sun; a crescent with stars); (b) also a tiny sun or crescent on
   the seal of a finished painting; (c) also the fill's sparkles become small stars at night;
   (d) another single place (say where).
6. **The finale.** Should finishing a painting sound different by day and by night? If so, one
   sentence each.
7. **Your jury's name.** The six of you will sit together on every new screen. Propose one name
   for yourselves, at most four words. You will rank the names in the next round.
