Step 1, after the itinerant signboard painters of Tiflis (Tiflis, c. 1870s-1900): Oil paint on sheet tin: a tavern or shop signboard. A Tiflis shop or dukhan signboard of the 1880s: oil paint on a sheet of tin, the thing on offer (a bull, a lamb, a fish) drawn large, flat and frontal in a few strong colours on a bright flat ground of blue, red or ochre, with a painted border and a band for lettering (a blur of Georgian and Russian letters, never legible). Hard-wearing, quickly painted, a little rusted at the edges. Keep it clearly a trade sign on tin, not a black oilcloth picture.
Step 2, after late-medieval Georgian church painting (Georgia, c. 12th-16th century): Earth pigments on lime plaster: a church wall painting. A Georgian church wall painting (Svaneti and Kakheti are the usual homes): ochre, red oxide, green earth, lamp-black and a deep blue ground laid flat on lime plaster, figures frontal and solemn with large calm eyes, bodies as simple coloured shapes outlined in dark paint, each scene on a plain band of colour. Its animals (the ox and ass of the Nativity, a lion, a stag) are drawn the same way: frontal or in profile, flat, still, not fussed over. A weathered wall, patches lost to damp.
Step 3, after medieval Georgian stone relief (Georgia, c. 7th-15th century): Carved stone relief (limestone or tuff) on a church wall or stele. A medieval Georgian stone relief: one deer (or a hunter's quarry) cut in low relief on a pale limestone slab or church facade, the body a smooth flat mass with a few incised lines for eye, antlers and legs, a plain recess behind it and a rope or plaited-vine border. Weathered, lichen-tinted, no colour. The animal fills the block, patient and frontal or in profile.
