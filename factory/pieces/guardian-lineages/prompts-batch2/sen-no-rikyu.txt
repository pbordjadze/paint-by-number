Guardian: Rikyū.
Essence (always keep): the one flower in the empty room: take things away until what is left matters, and let the empty space be part of the work. One bloom, set alone..
May echo, only where this tradition has its own native form for it, free in shape, scale and pose: one bloom, set alone; a wide margin of empty space around it.
Leave behind (by default): the small, uneven black tea bowl, tipped toward you; the morning glory on its rim; black raku glaze as a look; any 'Zen' pastiche: no imitation script, gongs or stereotyped speech (Originality's caution).

Step 1, after Takeno Jōō (Sakai, c. 1540s-1555). Medium: Unpainted cypress and green bamboo: a plain well-bucket used as a water jar, with a cut-bamboo lid-rest and a hand-carved bamboo scoop. What this tradition natively makes: Jōō's wabi tea: no lacquer, gold or Chinese bronze, but things from the house and the garden left plain. A well-bucket of pale unpainted cypress with its bamboo hoops and rope handle set on a tatami as the water jar; a lid-rest cut from a length of green bamboo, still glossy, its node showing; a scoop he carved himself from a slip of bamboo. One thing at a time, bare pale wood, and a great deal of empty mat; if a flower appears, one bloom in a plain bamboo section (a flower holder is not documented for Jōō himself).
Step 2, after Murata Jukō (made in Fujian, Southern Song (12th-13th c.); prized by Jukō (d. 1502)). Medium: Glazed stoneware: a Chinese celadon bowl of the kind named for him (Jukō celadon). What this tradition natively makes: A Jukō celadon bowl, about 15 cm across: Fujian folk-kiln celadon with a thick transparent glaze gone from jade-green to a warm loquat yellow-brown in an oxidising firing, grey-white body showing at the foot, the outside combed with fine 'cat-scratch' lines and the inside carved with a few free cloud shapes. A plain, slightly clumsy bowl, set alone on bare tatami or a plain tray, nothing beside it.
Step 3, after the Song tenmoku bowl (Fujian, Southern Song, 12th-13th c.; prized in Ashikaga Japan). Medium: Iron-glazed stoneware (Jian ware) bowl on a black lacquer stand. What this tradition natively makes: A Song Jian-ware tenmoku bowl: a deep conical wheel-thrown bowl of dense dark stoneware, thickly glazed brown-black with a glossy running glaze that pools in a dark bead above the foot, pale hare's-fur streaks and a rust-brown rim; set on a black lacquer stand with a flared foot, shown alone and centred, nothing else on the surface. Precise, glossy, sumptuous: the thing wabi tea replied to.
