Guardian: The Glassblower.
Essence (always keep): clear glass: one calm, transparent vessel that lets light through and holds a little inside it, native to its time and quiet.
May echo, only where this tradition has its own native form for it, free in shape, scale and pose: a pear- or drop-shaped hollow body narrowing to a neck, which is a blown bubble's own shape and is native to every step; light held inside a thin transparent wall.
Leave behind (by default): the face: two eyes and a smile; the curled tip as an outline; the scattered flecks of blue, rust, green and ochre; the watercolour rendering and the warm glow as a character; any Apple screen or Liquid Glass chrome.

Step 1, after Murano's cristallo (Murano, Venice, c. 1450-1500). Medium: Free-blown colourless soda glass (cristallo), hand-worked at the furnace; a goblet or bowl on a hollow stem and applied foot. What this tradition natively makes: A Murano cristallo vessel of the late 15th century: very thin, colourless, almost invisible glass blown free-hand into a simple pear or bell shape on a hollow stem and a folded foot, with at most a line of gilding or a few enamel dots at the rim. Its makers' pride is transparency 'like rock crystal', so show it with light passing through and nothing figured on it.
Step 2, after Mamluk Syrian enamelled glass (Syria and Egypt, 13th-14th century (the Met's lamp: shortly after 1285)). Medium: Blown glass, enamelled and gilded and fixed in the kiln: a mosque lamp. What this tradition natively makes: A Mamluk mosque lamp: a thin blown vessel with a bulbous body, a flaring neck and small loops for hanging chains, in pale clear glass banded with gilt and enamel (typically calligraphic friezes and rosettes in red, blue, white and gold), made to hold a flame. Gold and enamels were brushed or penned on and fired once; the light inside the glass is the whole point.
Step 3, after Roman mould-blown glass (Syro-Palestinian coast (Ennion is tied to Sidon), 1st century CE). Medium: Translucent honey-brown glass blown into a four-part mould, with an applied opaque-white handle: the Met's jug signed by Ennion. What this tradition natively makes: Ennion's jug: a pear-shaped body tapering to a flat base, a cylindrical neck, a folded rim and a ribbed handle in opaque white, in translucent deep honey-brown glass, its walls pressed by the mould with crisp ornament and signed in Greek. Show it as the real jug on a plain stand, the colour glowing where light passes through; it rhymes with the droplet by shape alone.
