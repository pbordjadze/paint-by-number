Guardian: The Snow Queen.
Essence (always keep): the hush: cold beauty made exact (stillness, precision, frost glimmer, restraint in colour). One quiet white presence. Today's drawing: a crowned white ermine..
May echo, only where this tradition has its own native form for it, free in shape, scale and pose: a small crown or ring of points; radial or mirror symmetry (the snow crystal's own form); drop where a tradition has no native form for it.
Leave behind (by default): the ermine and its black tail tip; folded, scissor-cut white paper (Andersen's own paper-cut manner); the diamond crown pierced with blue; white-on-cream paper rendering.

Step 1, after Soyuzmultfilm's The Snow Queen (Moscow, 1955-1957). Medium: Hand-painted animation cel: ink and paint on celluloid over a painted background. What this tradition natively makes: A Soyuzmultfilm cel of 1957: flat, hand-inked and gouache-painted figures against a softly painted backdrop; the Queen as a tall, still, stone-pale figure in a long pale-blue and white gown and a high crown, face composed and cold, in a palace of glassy ice with a few hard geometric crystals. Palette of white, ice-blue and silver with one small warm accent, stillness rather than action. Her movement in the film was traced from filmed footage of an actress, so keep the face generic: no likeness.
Step 2, after Andersen's tale, as first illustrated (Copenhagen, December 1849 (title page 1850); the text first appeared in 1844). Medium: Wood engraving printed in black on paper, in a letterpress book. What this tradition natively makes: A Danish Golden Age book of about 1850: black-line wood engraving on cream paper, fine hatching for shadow, no colour, set beside Danish roman text. The tale's own pictures of her are a big sleigh 'painted entirely white' driven by a figure in 'a white, shaggy fur cloak', and a window frozen 'as if they were covered with flowers'; show her only as that, a tall white-wrapped figure or a frosted pane. I did not see Pedersen's actual Snow Queen plate.
Step 3, after Hooke's Micrographia: the practice the tale stages (London, 1665 (after Kepler, 1611)). Medium: Copper engraving on a folio plate, printed in black on laid paper. What this tradition natively makes: A Micrographia plate of snow crystals: a copperplate engraving of many separate flakes, each a hexagon-based star drawn in fine engraved lines and tiny hatching, scattered on warm cream paper with a thin ruled border and plate letters. The Snow Queen exists here only as the exactness of her snow; no figure.
