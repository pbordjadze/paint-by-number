Guardian: Rikyū.
Essence: the one flower in the empty room: take things away until what is left matters, and let the empty space be part of the work. One bloom, set alone..
Rhymes that may echo: one bloom, set alone; a wide margin of empty space around it.

Step 1, after Takeno Jōō (Sakai, c. 1540s-1555): Unpainted cypress and green bamboo: a plain well-bucket used as a water jar, with a cut-bamboo lid-rest and a hand-carved bamboo scoop. Jōō's wabi tea: no lacquer, gold or Chinese bronze, but things from the house and the garden left plain. A well-bucket of pale unpainted cypress with its bamboo hoops and rope handle set on a tatami as the water jar; a lid-rest cut from a length of green bamboo, still glossy, its node showing; a scoop he carved himself from a slip of bamboo. One thing at a time, bare pale wood, and a great deal of empty mat; if a flower appears, one bloom in a plain bamboo section (a flower holder is not documented for Jōō himself).
Step 2, after Murata Jukō (made in Fujian, Southern Song (12th-13th c.); prized by Jukō (d. 1502)): Glazed stoneware: a Chinese celadon bowl of the kind named for him (Jukō celadon). A Jukō celadon bowl, about 15 cm across: Fujian folk-kiln celadon with a thick transparent glaze gone from jade-green to a warm loquat yellow-brown in an oxidising firing, grey-white body showing at the foot, the outside combed with fine 'cat-scratch' lines and the inside carved with a few free cloud shapes. A plain, slightly clumsy bowl, set alone on bare tatami or a plain tray, nothing beside it.
Step 3, after the Song tenmoku bowl (Fujian, Southern Song, 12th-13th c.; prized in Ashikaga Japan): Iron-glazed stoneware (Jian ware) bowl on a black lacquer stand. A Song Jian-ware tenmoku bowl: a deep conical wheel-thrown bowl of dense dark stoneware, thickly glazed brown-black with a glossy running glaze that pools in a dark bead above the foot, pale hare's-fur streaks and a rust-brown rim; set on a black lacquer stand with a flared foot, shown alone and centred, nothing else on the surface. Precise, glossy, sumptuous: the thing wabi tea replied to.
