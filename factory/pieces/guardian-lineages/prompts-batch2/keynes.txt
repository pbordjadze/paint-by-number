Guardian: Maynard.
Essence (always keep): the evening the money is for: money and machinery only the means, the point a good life with friends, beauty and talk. One small plump bird at ease..
May echo, only where this tradition has its own native form for it, free in shape, scale and pose: one small plump bird, only where a tradition natively makes one (a manuscript margin, the owl of Athens, the duck weight of Ur); a measure kept honest (a weight, a coin, a count).

Step 1, after the Schoolmen on usury (Latin West, late 13th-14th century). Medium: Ink and pigment on vellum: a scholastic manuscript of Aquinas's Summa theologiae (II-II, question 78, 'on the sin of usury'). What this tradition natively makes: A late-13th-century scholastic manuscript such as Bruges Public Library MS 563 (the Secunda secundae): two columns of tiny black Gothic textura on ruled vellum, red rubrics, red-and-blue pen-flourished initials, one decorated initial at the head of the question on usury, wide margins with a few small birds and leaf sprays drawn by the rubricator. Dense, plain and exact; the one decorated letter is the only gesture.
Step 2, after Aristotle's money (Athens, c. 450-404 BC). Medium: Struck silver: an Athenian tetradrachm. What this tradition natively makes: A silver tetradrachm of classical Athens, about 24 mm and 17 g, a thick, slightly irregular flan struck between two dies: on one face the helmeted head of Athena in profile with olive leaves on her helmet, on the other a plump owl standing and facing us, with an olive spray, a crescent and the letters ΑΘΕ; toned grey, a little worn at the edges. The bird is the whole value of the thing.
Step 3, after the duck weights of Ur (Ur or Nippur, c. 2100-2000 BC (Third Dynasty of Ur, Shulgi)). Medium: Polished dark stone: a duck-shaped weight with a short cuneiform inscription. What this tradition natively makes: An Ur III state weight: a smooth, dark, polished stone (diorite or pyroxenite) carved as a plump duck resting with its neck turned back along its body, the underside flat so it sits true, a short line of cuneiform incised on the back naming the king and the weight in minas. No paint, no ornament, fitted to the hand. A bird that is the standard.
