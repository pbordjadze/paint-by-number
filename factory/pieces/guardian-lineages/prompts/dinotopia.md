Guardian: Dino. Emblem to keep at every step: one plesiosaur in the approved pose: long neck curled back into an S, small head turned sideways looking at the viewer with a narrow, shifty, knowing eye, four paddle flippers spread, plump pale belly. The pose and that sly eye must be recognizable at every step.

Step 0 (today, faithful to the approved drawing): an open page of a 19th-century explorer's journal lying flat, the plesiosaur painted on it in careful watercolour and ink, blue-green with a cream belly, as in the approved drawing; a few faint pencil construction lines and the paper's texture. No legible writing beyond faint illegible pencil marks.
Step 1, after Charles R. Knight, 1897: a museum restoration painting in watercolour and gouache on paper board, lying flat: the same plesiosaur in the same pose, painted with Knight's naturalism: muted greys and olives, strong anatomy, a suggestion of water and sky behind, opaque gouache highlights.
Step 2, after the Crystal Palace dinosaurs, 1854: the same plesiosaur in the same pose as a Victorian sculpture of cast cement over brick and iron, painted in dull greenish-grey, life-size proportions but photographed as an object: visible cement texture, mould seams, weathering of real paint (not cracks added for effect), a rough cement base. The 1850s anatomy: a stiffer, longer neck, lizard-like head, but the S-curl and sideways sly eye kept.
Step 3, after Duria Antiquior, 1830: a lithograph print coloured by hand in watercolour, lying flat: lithographic crayon shading and fine hatching in black, thin washes of blue, green and buff added by hand, slightly outside the lines in places. The same plesiosaur in the same pose, drawn with the 1830 understanding of the animal (snaky neck, crocodile-like head), sly eye kept; a little sea around it.
