Guardian: the Moon. Emblem to keep at every step: a crescent moon with its horns pointing left, a calm profile face looking right on its inner curve, and a small lantern (a light) hanging from the lower horn. The crescent's shape and the light at its lower horn must be recognizable at every step.

Step 0 (today, faithful to the approved drawing): a single leaf from an early printed almanac lying flat, with the Moon as a woodcut: bold black cut lines, simple hatching, coloured by hand in yellow and a touch of red with the colour slightly outside the lines, the lantern with a warm flame. Laid paper texture. No legible text.
Step 1, the computus, England, late 11th to early 12th century: a vellum leaf from a reckoning book lying flat, with a ruled wheel of the moon's ages: concentric red and black rings with small numerals, in Romanesque pen drawing and light colour washes (green, red, ochre), and at the hub the crescent with its calm profile face, a small hanging lamp from its lower horn. No gold.
Step 2, Babylonian lunar reckoning, Uruk, c. 200 BC: a flat clay tablet photographed from above, its surface covered in small neat cuneiform wedges in ruled rows, and in its upper part a simple incised line drawing of the crescent (horns left, a few incised lines suggesting a face in profile) with a small incised lamp below its lower horn. Real fired clay: buff colour, small chips at the edges, wedge impressions catching the light.
Step 3, Sin's crescent and Nusku's lamp, Babylon, c. 1186-1172 BC: a grey limestone boundary stone (kudurru) shot straight on, its upper part carved in low relief with a crescent (horns pointing left), and right beside its lower horn the lamp of Nusku, carved as on real kudurrus: a small oil lamp on a stand. (Real kudurru crescents lie horns-up; here it is turned so its horns point left, to keep the emblem.) Carved, not painted. Real stone texture, ancient wear, no gold. No face on the crescent here except as a very faint suggestion, if any.
