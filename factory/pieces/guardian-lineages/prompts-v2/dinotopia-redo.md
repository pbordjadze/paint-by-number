Guardian: Dino. The shape: a long neck curling up and back into an S over a rounded body, four broad paddle-like limbs spread out below and to the sides.

IMPORTANT for both steps: the picture is a whole rectangular work on its paper or board, painted or printed edge to edge, lying flat. The shape must NOT be a cut-out or a silhouette filled with a scene, and not a double exposure. It appears INSIDE an ordinary-looking landscape as part of it, the way a figure hides in a real picture: it is recognisable once you know it, but the picture reads first as a seascape of its era.
Step 1, after Charles R. Knight, 1897: a museum restoration painting in watercolour and gouache on paper board, a full scene of a stormy prehistoric shore with sky, sea and distant cliffs. In the middle, one curling breaking wave rises into the S of the neck, its rounded swell is the body, and four fans of foam and backwash spread like paddles. Knight's naturalism: real light, muted greys, greens and ochres, opaque gouache highlights. No creature, no face.
Step 3, after Duria Antiquior, 1830: a lithograph drawn on stone with greasy crayon and coloured by hand in thin watercolour washes, a full scene with a printed border line, sky, sea and a cliffed shore. In the middle, a natural rock arch and sea stack curve into the S, a dark reef is the body, and four tongues of breaking surf spread like paddles. Grainy crayon tone, fine hatching, washes a little outside the lines. No creature drawn.
