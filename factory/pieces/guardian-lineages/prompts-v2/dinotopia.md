Guardian: Dino. The shape: a long neck curling up and back into an S over a rounded body, four broad paddle-like limbs spread out below and to the sides.
Step 1, after Charles R. Knight, 1897: a watercolour and gouache painting on paper board lying flat, in Knight's museum naturalism, but the shape is made of sea and weather: a curling wave and its spray form the S-neck, a dark swell the body, four spreading fans of foam the paddles. Real painted light and water; no creature, no face, no eye.
Step 2, after the Crystal Palace dinosaurs, 1854: a Victorian sculpture of cast cement ("artificial stone") over brick and iron, painted in dull slate and olive, shot as an object. The shape is the sculpture's mass, but reduced to its construction: the S-neck a curving tapered column of cement over an iron armature, the body a rounded mound with brick showing through where the render has fallen, four slabs for the paddles. A heavy built form, not a figurine; no face, no eye, no teeth.
Step 3, after Duria Antiquior, 1830: a lithograph drawn on stone with greasy crayon, coloured by hand in thin watercolour, lying flat. The shape is a coastal scene: a rock arch and sea stack curving into the S, a dark reef for the body, four tongues of breaking surf for the paddles, in lithographic crayon tone and hand washes. No creature drawn.
