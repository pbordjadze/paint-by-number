Guardian: the Moon. The shape: a crescent with its horns pointing left, its inner curve with a small profile notch at the middle, and a small upright block hanging below the lower horn on a short cord.
Step 1, the computus, England, late 11th to early 12th century: a vellum leaf of a reckoning book lying flat, Romanesque pen and light washes in green, red and ochre, Roman numerals only. The shape is made from the computus's own forms: the crescent is a curved band of ruled cells holding the moon's ages in Roman numerals (i to xxx), and hanging from its lower horn a small rectangular table of numbers in red and black. No face, no lamp.
Step 2, Babylonian lunar reckoning, Uruk, c. 200 BC: a fired clay tablet photographed from above, covered in neat cuneiform wedges. The shape is incised in the clay: a crescent formed by a curving band of wedges between two incised lines, and below its lower horn a small ruled box of wedges hanging from a short incised line. No face, no lamp, no pictures beyond lines and wedges.
Step 3, Babylon, c. 1186-1172 BC (a kudurru, the boundary stones carved with the gods' signs): a grey limestone boundary stone shot straight on, carved in low relief. The shape is carved as the stone's signs: a broad crescent (turned so its horns point left) and below its lower horn, hanging from a short carved cord, one small carved block-shaped emblem with simple incised lines. Plain cut stone, ancient wear, cuneiform lines beneath. No face, no lamp, no flame.
