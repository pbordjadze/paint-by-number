Guardian: Eno. The shape: a large circle and a smaller circle side by side, joined low by a slack curve; a thin stem with leaves rising from the large circle to a rounded five-lobed bloom; a small square tucked between the circles at the base.
Step 1, after John Cage, 1958 (Fontana Mix kind): sheets of paper and clear transparencies lying flat, overlaid. The shape is made only of chance-operation marks: the circles are fields of fine hand-drawn curved ink lines and scattered dots, the slack curve one wandering line, the stem a straight ruled line crossing a dot cluster for the bloom, the square a small patch of graph grid. No reels, no spools, no drawn objects.
Step 2, after Erik Satie, 1886-1900: one manuscript page, pen in black and red ink, elegant pseudo-Gothic calligraphy, music without bar lines. The shape is built from calligraphy: the two circles are rondels of written notes and lettering spiralling round, joined by one long pen flourish; the stem and bloom are a rising flourish ending in a rosette of red penwork; the square is a small block of ruled red lines. No reels, nothing mechanical.
Step 3, plainchant, 13th century: a parchment leaf of a chant book, black square neumes on red four-line staves, Gothic text. The shape is the page's decoration: two unequal round initial O's in red and blue penwork joined by a looping tendril, a sprig of penwork foliage rising from the larger, and at the base one large square neume. No reels, no dice.
