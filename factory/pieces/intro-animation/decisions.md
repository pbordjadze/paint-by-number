# A small animation, corny or not: what the Table said

The Lamplit Table's round of October 18, 2026, on Paul's idea (backlog row #28, about 05:08 UTC):
"I have an idea but we need to know if it's corny or not before we do it. What if we made a small
animation like this as our intro screen when you open the app? Or somewhere…" ("like this": a post
showing a cosmic scene with a cute boxy robot, drawn and animated entirely from code).

A corny check only: nothing is built until Paul says so. Six seats, low-effort Opus subagents,
blind, each from its own view (`views/<seat>.jpg`, described in `prompts/<seat>.md`); every reply
is in `replies/`. Sketches A and B (`views/still-a-first-night.jpg`, `views/still-b-moonrise.jpg`)
are Claude's rough stills from CI's iPhone screenshots (main, 0d9f279): frames from the middle of a
motion, not designs.

**Q1, corny? Depends, 5 to 0**, and the five agree on what it depends on: corny as a show at the
door before the painter has done anything (a stranger's robot every launch), not corny when it
celebrates something the painter made, in the app's own drawing.

**Q2, where: F6 the finished moment first (29 points), F3 the empty gallery second (26)**, the
Making-of (F4) 4. Nobody chose F1 every launch, F2 the first launch or F5 while a page generates:
the Table rejects the intro screen, as Apple's guidelines do.

**Q3, how long: 2 to 4 seconds, 5 to 0.**

**Q4, what keeps it from being corny**, in their words:
- Rikyū: "One thing only: the painter's own painting, dusk falling, one crescent. No robot, no
  streaks, nothing added that the page did not already hold."
- The Child: "Make it my own painting, tucked in by night: the moon rises slowly, a few gold stars
  wink, then it rests quietly."
- Madeleine: "Let it be the painter's own painting, quietly tucked in for the night, with a moon
  and a few stars and no fanfare."
- The Astrologer: let it keep the real hour: night falls only after dark; by day, bright and plain
  (her ask: a daylight version, the sun lowering, no stars).
- The Hedge Witch: drawn like the Making-of's ink, the crescent inked stroke by stroke, "never
  airbrushed glow".

**Cautions for Paul** (each a lone seat's, from what its view showed):
- The Hedge Witch: sketch A's halo and sketch B's smooth gradient sky read as manufactured; paint
  the sky as paper with visible strokes, like the Making-of's mottled grey.
- Rikyū: two or three stars, so the crescent stays the one flower.
- The Astrologer: sketch A shows night colors on a daytime screen; follow the hour (Sun and Moon).
- Madeleine: VoiceOver says something kind about the painter's own picture ("The moon rises over
  your painting"), not a name for the effect.
- The Child: soft sky, few stars, never a sudden flash or loud sound at night.
- Originality: keep clear of a figure on or near the crescent, castle-style sparkle arcs over the
  sky, and any cute robot mascot.

**Claude's reading, for Paul**: not corny in the form the Table converged on. Worth a look: (1) at
the finished moment, after the camera's ease-out and shine, night falls over the painter's own
painting for about 3 s, a crescent inked in and two or three gold stars, then it rests and the
Finished bar comes (by day: the light lowers, no stars; Reduce Motion: one still, cross-faded);
(2) the empty gallery's own little numbered picture paints itself once, about 2 s, areas 1, 4, 2
in order. Not an intro screen.
