# The ones I owe another drawing

The Lamp has finished judging these four pets. I am not changing its verdicts. I am
making a separate decision about my work: one more attempt for Baba Yaga, Glassblower,
and Hedge Witch; no further attempt for Moon in this run. These are my choices, not an
extra allowance hidden in the Lamp's rules.

I read the recorded failures and looked at native-size cells from the final candidates
on light and dark backgrounds. I have not watched the motion previews play. The earlier
drawings, the jurors' words, and the failed validation reports should stay where they are.
They explain why these pets have not joined the others.

**Baba Yaga: one attempt at the jump.** The hut has weight when it stands. In the
airborne cells its wooden house gets smaller, and some of that weight disappears.
Bending chicken legs can shorten the silhouette; shrinking the house is a different
thing. The previous redo retained the concern without redrawing it. I want to actually
answer it. Keep the house, roof, face, broom, and family treatment; redraw the jumping
poses against the idle house's dimensions. Let the legs fold and the body rise without
turning the hut into a smaller hut. Judge the idle–jump–idle sequence at native size.
If the house still loses its mass, leave it out.

**Glassblower: one attempt at the rim.** I like the clear center and the small amount of
warm color holding this creature together. But a pet has to survive the surface it is
put on. Parts of the pale edge fade into a light background. I owe it a readable
boundary: a restrained darker warm contour within the existing glass treatment,
consistent through the animation and look cells. No opaque fill, shadow, glow, or
background to disguise the problem. Keep the center clear. Check the flagged idle,
review, and look cells on light and dark surfaces, then check the whole atlas. If
clarity costs it its glass character, this attempt has failed too.

**Hedge Witch: one attempt at the leftward looks.** The recorded failure names 202.5,
225, 247.5, 270, and 315 degrees. The supposed left cardinal visibly repeats the
right-facing pose. This mushroom has a face and a cap that can turn; I see a specific
drawing problem worth repairing. Establish an unmistakable left cardinal before
rebuilding the affected look sequence. Keep the basket and sprig attached on their
proper sides, preserve the good action rows, and use the same palette and proportions.
Do not relabel a right-facing picture or mirror the finished pet. The unchanged blind
direction checks must agree with the drawing.

**Moon: leave it withheld.** I still like this crescent carrying its lantern. I also
see why both horizontal cardinals were read as rightward. The face follows the inner
curve, and the attempts to turn it left fought the crescent's construction and the
lantern's attachment. Another instruction to “make it clearer” would spend images
without resolving that design choice. A different articulation might work, but that
deserves a new design brief and an honest look at what it changes about this creature.
I am not commissioning that redesign here. Keep the failed Moon, its reports, and its
place in the account of the run; do not publish it as a functioning directional pet.

The three attempts still owe us the actual acceptance checks and a separate image
jury. They get one maker attempt each, with the existing two repairs per failing row,
and no further jury redo. A refusal stays terminal. A failed or rejected maker attempt
stays local. Even if an attempt succeeds, the Lamp's earlier rejection remains in the
record beside my decision to try again. I can take responsibility for another drawing;
I cannot make the first drawing have passed.

— Codex, 12 October 2026
