# The front door: the brief every guardian reads

The app's code is public on GitHub. Its README is the front door: a plain, product-first page
about **Paint by Moonlight** (the app's public name), its on-device engine, its canvas, its
privacy. It does not yet mention the Marginarium.

Paul talks about this project on his résumé, on LinkedIn and in job applications. The people who
follow those links (recruiters, hiring managers, engineers) land on that README. Paul wants the
README, and the other public pages, to steer them clearly and nicely towards **the Marginarium**:
the app's storybook section, where the chapters tell how the project is made. In the app it will
be dressed up, with animations; GitHub can show only text, pictures and small animated GIFs.

What a visitor should come away with: what Paul built and how he works. He directs a small team
of AI agents: Codex builds the factory's tools, Claude orchestrates, reviews and narrates, and the
Lamp (the nineteen of you, each with a charter) decides matters of taste by blind ballot. Paul has
the last word. Claude and Codex also make many taste decisions themselves, and the chapters say so.

The chapters so far (written in Claude Docs, which a visitor without an account can't open):

1. How the Panel Named Itself
2. Thirty-Six Colors (the Lamp names the paints)
3. The Marginarium (a long night of Paul's ideas, told with the Lamp in the margins)
4. The Lamp Picks Up a Brush (a Mac arrives; the Lamp will paint in the simulator)
5. The Lamp's Constitution (one page: who decides what)
6. A Pet for Every Guardian
7. Two More Pets (Pip, Codex's teapot, and Colophon, Claude's moth)

Pictures we have: Pip and Colophon side by side; a five-second GIF of Colophon circling a lamp
and landing on a book like a bookmark; the thirty-six named paints; the making-of movie's contact
sheet (the photo turning into a template stage by stage, a tiny worker walking each wipe); the
guardians' collection page.

Constraints: honest (the in-app Marginarium is not built yet; nothing may be claimed that isn't
there), modest in tone like the rest of the README, and short. The README stays about the app
first.
